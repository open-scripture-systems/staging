    The home page is now the home of Open Scripture Systems, the volunteer group behind this site, with a Join us page for people who read Japanese, people who read Greek, and designers. The list of tools, Word of God Workshop, has its own page; every earlier link still works.

  • 2026-11-23

    A logo for Open Scripture Systems, the group behind the Word of God Workshop: a ring made of the word "word" in Greek (ΛΟΓΟΣ), Hebrew (דבר), Aramaic (ܡܠܬܐ) and Japanese (御言葉, みことば, コトバ). It is in the top bar, the footer, the home page and the browser tab, and shows when a page is shared as a link.

  • 2026-11-20

    Study Workspace: in an Old Testament chapter the Hebrew now comes to the front where the Greek was (on a phone it is a tab next to the Japanese), and the Greek comes back in the New Testament. The New Testament review counts and the hint to click a Greek word no longer show on Old Testament chapters.

  • 2026-11-19

    In the Study Workspace's Greek interlinear, each word now shows its reading in katakana with the Latin letters in brackets, for example ロゴス (logos), on Japanese screens (a seminary professor asked for this). It follows the Erasmian reading many Japanese seminaries teach beginners and is a reading aid, not the authority on pronunciation; a button hides it.

  • 2026-11-19

    Plain names: the tool called Factbook is now People and Places in English (人名・地名事典 in Japanese, as before). Its address stays the same.

  • 2026-11-19

    The Kougo-yaku Old Testament (1955) is now in the Study Workspace too, with the same note as its New Testament: public domain in Japan, possibly still protected in the United States, and removed if the rights holder asks.

  • 2026-11-18

    Hebrew search in the Study Workspace: find an Old Testament word by its Hebrew (vowel points not needed), its transliteration, an English meaning or its Strong's number, see how often it is used, and open it in Hebrew Word Study.

  • 2026-11-18

    Hebrew Word Study in the Study Workspace: click a word in the Hebrew panel to see its lemma, transliteration, gloss, the STEPBible brief lexicon entry (from the abridged BDB), how often it is used, the books that use it most, and every place it occurs in the Old Testament, with the same word marked in the passage.

  • 2026-11-18

    The Old Testament is in the Study Workspace: all 39 books in the Hebrew (STEPBible TAHOT, CC BY 4.0, right to left with transliteration and glosses), the Bungo-yaku (Meiji translation, 1887), BSB, WEB and KJV. Pick a book from the menu or type a reference such as Psalm 23. The Kougo-yaku Old Testament is not on the site yet. Word Study and the other Greek tools stay New Testament only for now.

  • 2026-11-18

    Reading aid and a caution in the Study Workspace: the word card and Word Study show each Greek word in katakana and Latin letters (the Erasmian way many Japanese seminaries teach, marked as a guide only), and Word Study lists three mistakes to avoid: meaning from a word's parts, all meanings at once, and later meanings read back. The word card's Greek also uses the Greek font now.

  • 2026-11-18

    New panel in the Study Workspace: Structure. The Greek of the chapter is laid out in clause lines, in text order, with a clause that does a job inside another set one step in, from MACULA Greek's syntax trees (CC BY 4.0). Move any line in or out to make your own outline, kept on your device, and save it as a file.

  • 2026-11-18

    New panel in the Study Workspace: Reading plan. A teacher lists chapters, with dates if wanted, and shares the plan as a file or a link; each student ticks off what they have read on their own device, and a click opens the chapter. A range like John 2-3 becomes two readings. Nothing is sent to this site, and a teacher does not see who read what.

  • 2026-11-18

    New panel in the Study Workspace: Grammar terms. Every Greek grammar label the workspace shows (for example imperfect, middle voice, participle) with a one-line plain explanation in Japanese and English, and the places in the chapter in view that carry it; click one to select that word. The explanations are a draft until a teacher checks them.

  • 2026-11-18

    Guided study for classes: three more studies (word study in the original language, passage exegesis, a short reading and prayer), all marked as drafts until a pastor or teacher checks them. A teacher can now write a study in the browser, save it as a file or copy a link that carries it; a student who opens the link gets the study and the passage on their own device. Students can print their answers or save a hand-in file, which the teacher opens read only. Nothing is sent to this site.

  • 2026-11-18

    The volunteer review of the draft Kougo-yaku links now has a Send button at the end: the answers reach the site's private Google Form directly, so nobody has to copy and paste them into an email.

  • 2026-11-18

    Link sets in the Study Workspace: a Bible text or the Greek panel can now follow set B or C instead of A, so two or three passages stay open side by side. Click the letter on a panel's tab to change its set, or type "set B" in the command box. The top bar moves the set of the panel used last. Word Study, notes and the other study panels stay with set A.

  • 2026-11-18

    New panel in the Study Workspace: Word details. Rest the mouse on a Greek word (or move the keyboard focus to it) and the panel shows its lemma, parsing, gloss, New Testament count, the books that use it most, its part in the sentence and the start of the lexicon entry, without a click. A button keeps one word in view.

  • 2026-11-18

    In the Study Workspace's Greek interlinear, a new switch shows the parts of each sentence under the words: subject, verb, object, indirect object and predicate, from MACULA Greek (CC BY 4.0).

  • 2026-11-18

    Added Word use to the Study Workspace: for a Greek verb, the words most often its subject and its object across the New Testament, counted from MACULA Greek (CC BY 4.0). Open it from the word card or the left menu.

  • 2026-11-18

The list can be saved as a file.
